Method overview

Abstract

Diffusion Transformers (DiTs) have achieved remarkable performance in video synthesis, but their iterative denoising process suffers from high inference latency. Caching has emerged as an effective acceleration strategy by exploiting inter-step redundancy during denoising. Existing dynamic caching methods typically estimate the error that cache reuse would introduce at each denoising step (step error) to guide cache decisions, whereas our concern is how much fidelity loss cache reuse would cause in the final generated video (terminal error). We show that step error does not directly correspond to terminal error and latent information helps capture their relationship, thereby informing cache decisions. Moreover, these methods rely on threshold-based rules that do not provide precise speedup control, making it difficult to meet user-specified acceleration targets. To address these limitations, we introduce MORCA, a cache scheduling framework trained through offline-to-online reinforcement learning to make latent-aware reuse/recompute decisions under user-specified acceleration targets. Extensive experiments on different video generation models across multiple speedup targets demonstrate that MORCA achieves better speed–fidelity trade-offs than state-of-the-art caching methods.

Motivation

Existing dynamic caching methods use step error—the immediate error introduced by cache reuse—to guide their decisions. Our goal, however, is to minimize terminal error: the quality loss in the final generated video.

Step error does not directly correspond to terminal error. On Wan2.1, step error follows similar patterns across prompts, while the resulting terminal error varies substantially. A local error signal alone is therefore insufficient to guide decisions about final video quality.

Latent information improves terminal-error prediction. As shown below, adding lightweight features from the current and previous latents improves prediction over using step error alone. This motivates combining latent features with step-error signals in our cache scheduler.

Budget-Constrained and Latent-Aware Scheduler

Latent-aware scheduling. Our motivation study shows that step error alone is insufficient to predict terminal error, while latent information improves prediction. We therefore design a lightweight scheduler that combines latent features with step-error proxies and reuse-budget information, helping it make reuse/recompute decisions based on the quality of the generated video.

From target speedup to reuse budget. Given a user-specified target speedup, we use our calibrated relationship between speedup and the number of reuse steps to determine a reuse budget. The scheduler then selects which denoising steps to reuse while satisfying this budget.

Offline-to-Online Reinforcement Learning Paradigm

Offline training. We collect trajectories using randomly sampled cache schedules across prompts and target speedups, then use Implicit Q-Learning (IQL) to learn an initial scheduler from this pre-collected dataset.

Online fine-tuning. Starting from the offline-trained scheduler, we collect new trajectories with the current policy and add them to a replay buffer alongside offline and earlier online data. A short critic warm-up is followed by joint value-function and policy updates using the same IQL procedure, enabling further exploration and policy improvement.
